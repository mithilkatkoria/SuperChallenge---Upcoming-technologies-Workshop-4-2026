QUANTUM COMPUTING
The next leap beyond the limits of classical processors
Normal computers have a problem: there is a physical limit to how powerful a single processor can get. Wires can only get so thin, and electricity can only travel so fast. That is why scientists are looking beyond silicon entirely. Quantum computing uses the strange laws of quantum physics to process information in a completely different way, and it could be one of the biggest breakthroughs in the history of technology.
Classical Bit
0 or 1
A normal computer bit is either on or off, nothing in between. Every device you own runs on billions of these simple switches flipping back and forth incredibly fast.
Quantum Qubit
0 + 1
A qubit can be both 0 and 1 at the same time. Just 8 qubits can represent all 256 possible values at once, rather than just one at a time like a normal computer.
Three Key Principles
🌀
Superposition
Qubits exist in multiple states at once. Think of it like a coin spinning in the air before it lands. This lets quantum computers look at many possible answers at the same time.
🔗
Entanglement
Two qubits can be linked so that what happens to one instantly affects the other, no matter how far apart they are. Einstein called this "spooky action at a distance."
〰️
Interference
Quantum algorithms use wave patterns to boost the right answers and cancel out the wrong ones, steering the computer towards the correct solution much faster.
How It Works
STEP 01
Freeze It
Qubits are cooled to near absolute zero, colder than outer space, to hold their quantum state
›
STEP 02
Superpose
Qubits enter superposition, exploring all possible states at once rather than one at a time
›
STEP 03
Entangle
Qubits are linked together so their states are connected, enabling far more complex calculations
›
STEP 04
Measure
The quantum state collapses into a normal result and the answer is extracted from the computation
Real World Uses
💊
Drug Discovery
Simulating how molecules behave could help scientists find cures for diseases like Alzheimer's and cancer far faster than today
🔐
Cryptography
Quantum computers could break today's internet encryption, but also create completely unbreakable quantum-secured communication
🧠
AI and Machine Learning
Processing huge, complex datasets much faster could make AI systems dramatically more powerful and accurate
📦
Optimisation
Solving huge logistics problems, like the most efficient route for thousands of delivery drivers, in seconds rather than days

Current Challenges
⚠
Decoherence Qubits are incredibly fragile. Even tiny vibrations or heat cause them to lose their quantum state and stop working
⚠
Error Rates Today's quantum computers make lots of mistakes and need complicated error correction just to stay reliable
⚠
Scaling Up Useful applications need thousands of stable qubits, but current machines have fewer than 100 that work reliably
